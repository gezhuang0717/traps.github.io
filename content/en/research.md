## Scientific background

**Penning-trap mass spectrometry.** An ion of charge *q* and mass *m* in a strong magnetic field *B* and a weak electrostatic quadrupole potential performs three eigenmotions; the sum of the reduced-cyclotron and magnetron frequencies is, to very good approximation, the free cyclotron frequency *ν*<sub>c</sub> = *qB*/(2π*m*). Comparing *ν*<sub>c</sub> of the ion of interest with that of a reference ion of well-known mass cancels the magnetic field, so the mass follows from a frequency ratio. At JYFLTRAP a first trap purifies the ion bunch by buffer-gas cooling and a second trap performs the measurement; relative uncertainties of 10<sup>−8</sup> and below are reachable for short-lived nuclei. In the time-of-flight ion-cyclotron-resonance (TOF-ICR) method the excitation frequency is scanned and the resonance appears as a minimum of the time of flight to the detector; in phase-imaging ion-cyclotron-resonance (PI-ICR) the radial phase accumulated in a fixed time is projected onto a position-sensitive detector, which gives a higher resolving power and resolves low-lying isomeric states.

**Multi-reflection time-of-flight (MR-TOF) mass spectrometry.** Ions are reflected many times between two electrostatic mirrors, which lengthens the flight path to hundreds of metres in a compact device. The resolving power *R* = *m*/Δ*m* = *t*/(2Δ*t*) grows with the flight time, so that isobars become separable within milliseconds; the instrument serves as an isobar separator, a mass spectrometer and a beam diagnostic. It is calibrated with reference ions, because *m*/*q* is proportional to (*t* − *t*<sub>0</sub>)<sup>2</sup>.

**Decay energies and the neutrino mass.** The *Q*-value of a β⁻ decay or an electron capture is the difference of the atomic masses of parent and daughter, *Q* = [*M*(parent) − *M*(daughter)]*c*<sup>2</sup>. Direct neutrino-mass experiments analyse the region near the end point of the spectrum, where a very small *Q* increases the sensitivity. Penning-trap measurements of *Q* with sub-keV precision find such ultra-low-*Q* transitions and exclude candidates that do not qualify.

**Nuclear structure.** Binding energies derived from masses show shell closures (a drop of the two-neutron separation energy after a magic number), pairing (odd–even staggering) and the extra binding of *N* = *Z* nuclei; mirror nuclei test isospin symmetry through Coulomb displacement energies. ¹⁰⁰Sn is the heaviest self-conjugate nucleus expected to be doubly magic.

**Nuclear astrophysics.** In X-ray bursts and the νp-process the reaction flow runs close to the proton drip line and waits at nuclei with small proton-capture *Q*-values; in the r-process the neutron-separation energies fix the path of the neutron-capture flow. Masses are therefore central nuclear input for the origin of the elements; see the panel [Periodic table & origin of the elements](../games/#g-ptable).

**Isotope shifts.** In laser spectroscopy of isotope chains the mass shift requires accurate atomic masses; they reduce the uncertainty of the charge-radius differences extracted from the measured shifts.
